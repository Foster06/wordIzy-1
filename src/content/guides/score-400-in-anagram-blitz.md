Anagram Blitz is WordIzy's free 60-second word unscramble game, and scoring 400 points in a single round puts you in the top 5 percent of all players. Each correct answer earns 20 points, each skip costs 5, and every correct answer in Infinite Mode adds 10 bonus seconds to the clock. That means scoring 400+ requires netting roughly 20 correct answers in 60 seconds — about one solve every three seconds, with no wasted time. This guide breaks down the seven specific techniques that top players use to hit 400+ consistently, including the mental shortcuts that let you recognize anagram patterns before you've even finished reading the letters on screen.

## Tip 1: Memorize the Most Common Suffixes First

The single fastest way to recognize a scrambled English word is to spot its suffix. English anagrams overwhelmingly end in -S, -ED, -ING, -ER, -LY, -E, -TION, or -ABLE. Train your eye to scan the letter set for these suffixes the instant the puzzle appears — if you see an E, D, and a couple of consonants, immediately try -ED endings. If you see I, N, G, lock those three letters as -ING first and figure out the rest of the word. Roughly 40 percent of all five-letter Wordle answers and Anagram Blitz solutions end in one of just eight suffix patterns. If you spot the suffix in one second, the remaining 4 letters are usually obvious.

## Tip 2: Vowels First, Consonants Second

When a letter set appears, your brain naturally tries to identify the consonants first because they carry more meaning. That's wrong for speed unscramble. Train yourself to count the vowels first — most English words have one or two vowels per three consonants, so if you see 7 letters with 2 vowels, the vowels are likely spaced apart. Lock in the vowel positions early (typically positions 2 and 4 in a 5-letter word, or 2 and 5 in a 7-letter word), then experiment with consonant placements. The vowel-first habit shaves a full second off your average solve time, which translates to 15-20 extra correct answers per game.

## Tip 3: Type Without Hesitation — Trust Your First Instinct

The biggest score-killer in Anagram Blitz is the half-second you spend second-guessing an answer that's actually correct. If a word forms in your head, type it and hit enter immediately — even if you're only 80 percent sure. A wrong answer costs zero points (just one wasted second), but a hesitated correct answer costs the time you spent double-checking. Top players type the first plausible answer they see, accepting that some will be wrong, because the math favors speed over accuracy. If you're getting 7 out of 10 right and averaging 3 seconds per solve, you'll net 140 points; if you slow down to 90 percent accuracy at 5 seconds per solve, you'll only net 220 — the speed loss outweighs the accuracy gain.

## Tip 4: Skip Wrong-Looking Puzzles Fast

Every second spent staring at a puzzle you can't solve is a second you can't get back. The 5-point skip penalty is trivial compared to the time cost — a 10-second stall on a hard puzzle costs you ~60 points of potential scoring (you could have answered 3 more puzzles in that time). Set a hard 3-second rule: if you can't form a plausible answer in 3 seconds, hit Skip immediately and move on. The puzzle generator occasionally produces letter sets that are genuinely hard — obscure loan words, 7-letter bingos from rare Scrabble dictionary entries, or letter combos that don't form common words. Skip those without remorse and you'll keep your scoring pace up.

## Tip 5: Use Daily Challenges to Train Under Pressure

The Daily Challenge gives you one puzzle per day with a single attempt, locked at midnight. Use it as your high-pressure training ground — the locked single attempt forces you to focus and play your best, which carries over into your Infinite Practice performance. Track your daily scores in a notes app or spreadsheet for two weeks and watch them climb as you internalize the patterns. Most players gain 50-100 points in their first month of focused daily play. The Daily Challenge also uses a curated word list that mirrors the most common words in Infinite Mode, so anything you learn in the daily directly transfers.

## Tip 6: Choose Your Letter Sets Wisely in Infinite Mode

Infinite Practice Mode lets you influence which puzzles you see — your rack balance affects which letters appear. Aim for racks that contain at least one S, E, R, or T (the most flexible English letters), and avoid racking up Q, Z, X, J unless you're confident in your high-value word vocabulary. Most Anagram Blitz solutions are 4-6 letters long, so prioritize shorter, common words over long bingo attempts.

## Tip 7: Build a Personal "Pattern Recall" Library

Elite players drill common anagram families outside of game time. The 20 most common Anagram Blitz solutions are: SLATE, STALE, TALES, LEAST, STEAL, TASER, RATEL, ALERT, ALTER, LATER, RECIT, TERSE, TREES, STEER, RETS, REST, PASTE, PEATS, SPATE, TAPES. Drill those 20 words until you can identify any scrambled version in under 2 seconds. Use the WordIzy **Save Vault** to bookmark tricky ones and export to Anki flashcards for spaced-repetition review.

## Closing: Practice the Right Way

Scoring 400+ consistently takes about 20 focused hours of practice using these techniques. Don't expect it in week one — the pattern recognition takes time to internalize. But by week three you'll start recognizing suffixes automatically, your typing speed will catch up to your thinking, and 400+ will become your new baseline.
